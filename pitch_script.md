## 🎯 10 Likely Judge Questions & Evidence-Backed Answers

### Q1: *"Why shouldn't we open both Wellington Place and Kelham Island right now if we have the £1.5M?"*
**Answer:**  
"Because Leeds Briggate taught us a severe £228,000 lesson. When Leeds Trinity opened 0.4 miles away, Briggate lost 28.2% of its footfall and £19,000 a month in sales [Measured]. If you open Kelham Island and Wellington Place simultaneously while your core stores are hemorrhaging staff and wasting food, you dilute management bandwidth across broken operational foundations. Wellington Place is uniquely timed for Autumn 2026 to capture 2,000 incoming office workers, but Kelham Island should be deployed in FY2027 once Wellington Place's cash flows are proven. Holding £775,000 in reserve protects your balance sheet."

### Q2: *"Won't an £8 minimum basket on delivery alienate customers and hurt our sales?"*
**Answer:**  
"RushEats' own partnership data from 22 April proves the opposite. Their account manager noted that across coffee partners who instituted an £8 basket or a £1.50 small-order fee, average order value rose by 18% to 25% with only a negligible dip in order volume. In our transaction data, an average sub-£8 order is £4.87 [Sampled]. At 33% Dashly commission plus £1.39 in packaging and staff packing time, Grind & Crumb retains only 41 pence before food cost. If a customer drops off, you lose almost zero profit; if they add a £3.10 cinnamon bun to reach £8, you gain 38% contribution margin. It is a win-win."

### Q3: *"Can 2 extra baristas at Piccadilly really generate £53,000 in net profit?"*
**Answer:**  
"Yes, and the math is conservative. Piccadilly has 71,000 daily street footfall and 127,768 annual lunch visitors [Measured]. It currently operates at 27 visitors per labour hour versus the chain average of 19. Both the manager and barista state that when the queue hits 5 deep, customers walk away to station kiosks. Recovering just 25 transactions a day at an £8.50 lunch basket yields £77,500 in sales, or £54,300 gross margin net of COGS [Assumed]. Furthermore, Piccadilly had 53 leavers this year (230% turnover) [Measured], with 50% citing understaffing. Cutting that churn by 40% saves £25,200 in hiring and training costs. Against a £26,400 labour cost, the net return is £53,200 a year."

### Q4: *"Why do you propose refitting York Stonegate when it's already our most profitable store?"*
**Answer:**  
"Because York Stonegate is your golden goose, and it is 7 years old without a single refit [Measured]. York generates a 46.8% margin and £413,000 in annual profit [Measured]. In retail hospitality, tired fixtures reduce dwell time and tourist conversion. In your `capex.csv` dataset, when Sheffield Division Street was refitted for £140k in 2023 and Briggate for £210k in 2024, both saw sustained 7% to 9% revenue uplifts. A modest 7.5% uplift on York's £888k revenue generates £31,200 in net profit every year, fully paying back in 4.8 years while preserving your highest-margin brand asset."

### Q5: *"What went wrong with App v3.0, and how do you know it wasn't just a seasonal dip?"*
**Answer:**  
"We verified it against two independent controls. First, while overall chain sales in March were steady, App channel revenue plummeted by 47.5% from £25.5k/week to £13.4k/week immediately following the 2 March release [Measured]. Second, customer review ratings dropped from 3.80 to 3.45 stars [Measured], with 14.3% of all reviews during that window explicitly citing checkout freezes on older phones and lost loyalty stamps [Sampled]. Customer survey responses in May confirmed this verbatim: *'Fix the app, my stamps vanished.'* Our allocation includes a £15,000 customer goodwill fund to restore lost stamps to 3,200 affected loyalty accounts, rebuilding digital order run-rates."

### Q6: *"How did you handle the fact that transactions.csv is only a 10% sample?"*
**Answer:**  
"We maintained strict data hygiene. All financial totals—chain sales of £8,656,692.49, channel sales, and store monthly P&Ls—were calculated exclusively from `daily_store_sales.csv` and `store_costs_monthly.csv` [Measured]. We used `transactions.csv` and `transaction_lines.csv` strictly to extract distributions and ratios: the 29.0% share of sub-£8 orders, the partner split between RushEats and Dashly, and the 7.4 percentage point drop in oat milk share. We never multiplied sample line items to represent revenue without explicit normalization."

### Q7: *"Did you spot any data anomalies in the provided files?"*
**Answer:**  
"Yes, we identified and resolved eight specific data anomalies, all documented in `data_issues.md`:
1. `waste_log.csv` had 273 rows in October 2025 formatted as `DD/MM/YYYY` instead of `YYYY-MM-DD`. Naive parsing corrupts monthly totals.
2. `transactions.csv` had 64 duplicate rows and 570 refund rows with `-RF` suffixes.
3. `transaction_lines.csv` had 1,665 negative line items—all belonging to `MEALDEAL` promotional bundle deductions.
4. `loyalty_customers.csv` had 25 duplicate customer records.
5. `waste_log.csv` had 129 nulls in `units_wasted`, which we imputed using unit cost ratios.
6. `staff_rota_weekly.csv` spanned 53 weeks, requiring pro-rated daily calendar allocation.
7. `customer_reviews.csv` used unstructured store aliases which we mapped via fuzzy matching.
8. Garbled UTF-8 currency encodings (`Â£`) in `business_events.csv`."

### Q8: *"Should we drop the £0.40 oat milk charge like Bean Social did?"*
**Answer:**  
"Our recommendation is to **retain the charge in affluent locations, but bundle it into loyalty rewards**. The oat milk charge generates approximately £104,000 in annualised revenue [Assumed]. While oat milk share dropped from 31.2% to 23.8% [Sampled] and drew negative reviews, eliminating it entirely would leave a £104,000 margin hole. Instead, our app recovery strategy allows loyalty members to redeem digital stamps for free plant milk modifiers, neutralizing competitor Bean Social's advantage without surrendering margin from casual transit customers."

### Q9: *"How does the Dynamic Par Level Tool actually work for store managers?"*
**Answer:**  
"Currently, store managers like Marcus Dyer at Hull and Grace Okafor at Briggate use static mental targets or last year's par sheets. The tool we built pulls daily historical transaction patterns, adjusts for day-of-week seasonality, incorporates local weather forecasts (rain reduces high street footfall by 12% in our weather data), and factors in nearby store cannibalisation. It generates a suggested daily bake sheet for morning, lunch, and afternoon bakery and sandwich prep, targeting a 9.0% waste rate rather than Hull's current 20.6%."

**Answer:**

In Year 2, steady-state operational savings plus Wellington Place maturity generate **£404,000** in net profit. In Year 3, upon deploying the £775,000 reserve into Sheffield Kelham Island, annual net benefit exceeds **£560,000**. Over 3 years, Grind & Crumb recovers **£1,244,000 in cumulative net operating cash flow** on a £1.5M investment, transforming chain store profit from £3.2M to £3.76M annually."
